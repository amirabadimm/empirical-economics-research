\begin{abstract}
This report estimates the premium or discount of the Iranian iron-ore pellet warehouse receipt
relative to comparable domestic physical pellet trades. The benchmark uses Gol Gohar and Gohar
Zamin, accepts only strict-cash transactions, and compares prices on exact overlapping dates.
The 22-observation exploratory sample has a mean bubble of $-12.18\pct$ and only one positive
observation. The result is informative but composition-sensitive because 16 observations use a
single producer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research Question and Scope}
The study asks whether the pellet warehouse receipt trades at a premium or discount to a
defensible domestic physical benchmark. Global prices, exchange rates, and external
intrinsic-value models are outside scope. Raw source files and immutable snapshots are never
modified by analysis.

\section{Data and Governance}
Certificate data come from the Iran Mercantile Exchange CDC endpoint. Contract codes
\texttt{CD1IOP0001} and \texttt{IronOrePlt} form a continuous series beginning on 1404/07/28.
\texttt{TodaySettlementPrice} is the analytical certificate price; value divided by volume is a
rounding-tolerant validation only.

Physical-market data come from the official IME statistics endpoint. The raw collector retains
the broad exact-goods-name scope. Producer, contract, settlement, price, and quantity rules are
applied only in the analytical layer. Collectors are incremental, idempotent, and atomic; raw
snapshots are immutable and excluded from Git.

\section{Producer Selection}
Selection is based on all positive physical trades since certificate inception, regardless of
contract and settlement type. Gol Gohar represents 30.75\pct{} and Gohar Zamin 23.10\pct{}, for
a combined 53.86\pct{}. Chadormalu represents 9.81\pct{} and Sangan Khorasan 5.66\pct{} and are
excluded from the candidate benchmark because of lower representation. The decision affects
analysis only and does not remove source observations.

\section{Benchmark Construction}
Eligible rows must have a cash or cash-matching contract, an explicitly cash settlement, and
positive executed price and quantity. Cash/credit trades are excluded. A date is eligible when at
least one selected producer trades. A single-producer date uses that observed price; a
two-producer date uses the simple mean. Certificate comparisons require an exact-date overlap and
positive certificate volume and price. The study uses neither interpolation nor carry-forward.

\section{Certificate Bubble}
For eligible date $t$, the bubble is
\[
B_t = 100\left(\frac{P^{cert}_t}{P^{physical}_t}-1\right).
\]
The mean is $-12.18\pct$, the median is $-11.55\pct$, the minimum is $-24.93\pct$, and the
maximum is $+11.03\pct$. Only one observation is positive.

\section{Positive-Bubble Case Study}
On 1404/10/21, the qualifying Gol Gohar cash price is IRR 94,566/kg and the certificate settles
at IRR 104,998/kg, producing a $+11.03\pct$ bubble. Gohar Zamin trades at IRR 111,229/kg but is
excluded because settlement is cash/credit. A sensitivity mean using the two same-day producer
prices reduces the bubble to approximately $+2.04\pct$, demonstrating material composition risk.

Certificate settlement had already risen from IRR 81,844/kg on 1404/10/10 to IRR 103,483/kg on
1404/10/17. The positive observation is therefore consistent with a timing difference in price
discovery, but available data do not establish a causal explanation.

\section{Limitations and Research Status}
The series is exploratory. Sixteen of 22 dates rely on one producer, so the magnitude of the
bubble is sensitive to benchmark composition. Findings should always separate single- and
two-producer dates and report sensitivity checks. A production processed benchmark should be
created only after the selection rule receives final approval and time coverage expands.
